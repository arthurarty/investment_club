The impact of savings clubs

Uganda's most trusted bank often meets under a mango tree.

Long before mobile money, neighbours pooled their savings to look after each other. Today savings clubs are the most common way Ugandans save and borrow, reaching families that banks rarely serve.

Children stay in school

Term fees arrive three times a year. A club loan or a planned share-out means a child goes back to class instead of staying home.

Farms and businesses grow

Small loans buy seed, fertiliser, a second sewing machine or stock for a duka, and members repay from what they earn.

Families get through hard times

Welfare funds cover hospital bills, funerals and failed harvests, so one bad month doesn't wipe out years of progress.

Women lead

Women make up most savings group members and many of their chairpersons and treasurers, gaining a voice in household and community decisions.

Homes get built

Year-end share-outs turn steady weekly savings into iron sheets, bricks, land and livestock.

A first step into finance

A club's track record is often a member's first credit history, and a path to SACCOs, banks and bigger opportunities.

The record keeping gap

Clubs run on trust, and trust runs on good records.

Most clubs still track contributions, loans and interest in exercise books and passbooks. When a book goes missing or a figure doesn't add up, it's the members' confidence that suffers. Very few groups keep their records digitally today.

What SavingsInc does

The treasurer's book, without the late nights.

One shared, always-balanced record for the whole club, which every member can trust and the officials can manage in minutes.

Contributions & savings

Record every member's deposit and see running balances per member and for the club. Members get an email receipt for each contribution.

Loans & repayments

Track who borrowed, how much, the interest due and what's been repaid, so there are no disputes at the next meeting.

Expenses

Log club spending, from meeting refreshments to bank charges, with a clear category and approver for each.

Monthly & yearly reports

Monthly reports emailed to every member, plus financial-year summaries ready for share-out day.
